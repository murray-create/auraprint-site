Choosing outerwear that gets worn

Fleece weight is the whole decision

A 290gsm fleece is light, soft and suits a mild Sunshine Coast winter or an air conditioned office. A 310gsm cotton rich fleece like the JB’s 3FH is the workwear standard and takes a beating. At 320gsm and above you are into premium merch territory, where the garment feels substantial enough that people will pay for it. Heavier is not automatically better here. On the Coast, a 350gsm hoodie gets worn about six weeks a year, and a softshell gets worn far more.

Softshell is the quiet winner for business

For most corporate and trade uniform programmes the three layer softshell does more work than a hoodie. It is wind resistant, it layers over a polo, it looks appropriate at a client site, and it embroiders beautifully because the face fabric is smooth. The JB’s 3WSJ runs from 2XS to 5XL in adults plus a kids run, with a matching ladies cut, which covers a whole organisation in one style.

Embroidery or print on outerwear

Embroidery is the default on softshell, puffers and corporate fleece, because the finish matches the garment and it lasts. On cotton fleece hoodies you have a real choice. Embroidery on the left chest reads as uniform. A large printed centre chest or full back reads as merch. Plenty of orders do both, with a small embroidered chest logo and a printed back. Just be aware that each position is a separate setup and outerwear decoration areas are larger, so costs move faster here than on a tee.

Puffers are a gifting garment, not a uniform garment

A branded puffer is what businesses hand to clients and long serving staff rather than issue to a crew. They photograph well, they feel like a real gift, and a small tonal chest logo suits them better than a large one. If you are considering them as a staff garment, count the number of genuinely cold days on the Coast first, then decide whether a softshell would be worn more.

Who orders outerwear here

Trade crews

Early winter starts. Hi vis hoodies where the site requires it, plain fleece where it does not, with the business name across the back.

Corporate and professional services

Softshell jackets in the company colour with a small chest logo. The layer that goes over the corporate polo or shirt.

Schools and clubs

Year twelve jerseys, club hoodies and supporter gear. Usually a large back print with names or numbers, which suits DTF.

Cafes, gyms and local brands

Merch hoodies people actually buy. Heavier weight garments, printed rather than embroidered, in a limited colour run.

Client and staff gifting

Puffers and premium softshells at end of year or on a milestone. Small tonal embroidery, quality over volume.

Events and expos

Stand crews in matching softshells. Worth ordering with the banners and tablecloths so the whole stand arrives together.

How ordering works

Tell us what you need

Pick the garments below and send them through, or just describe the job. Headcount, rough sizes, how many logo positions and your deadline are enough to start.

We quote it properly

We price the garment, the decoration and the freight to your address as one number, and we confirm the real minimum order and lead time for your job rather than a general one.

Approve your mockup

You get a visual showing your logo in position and at actual size on the garment you chose. Nothing is stitched or pressed until you approve it.

We produce and deliver

As a guide, ten to fifteen business days from artwork approval, plus freight. The clock starts at approval, not at order, and supplier stock is the usual cause of a delay.

How we supply uniforms

We do not hold garment stock or run embroidery machines at Baringa. Uniforms and merch are produced through established Australian trade decorators who hold the garment brands, do the embroidery and printing, and dispatch direct. You deal with us, we deal with them, and the job arrives without their name on it.

That is deliberate. It means we can put you on the right garment from any of the major brands rather than the one brand we happen to stock, and it means a small order is not penalised for being small. It also means we quote rather than show a live price on these pages, because the honest number depends on the garment, the quantity, the number of decoration positions and how many stitches your logo needs.

Minimums and lead times, honestly

Every number on this page is a guide, and we confirm the real one on your quote. As a rough guide: embroidery from around 10 to 12 units on a new logo and often a single garment on a reorder, screen printing from around 20 to 25 per design per colour, and DTF from about 10 with no real technical minimum. Caps usually hold a minimum of around 12 even on repeats.

Lead time is typically 10 to 15 business days from the moment you approve the artwork, plus freight to you. Two things get missed. The clock starts at artwork approval and not at order placement, and garment stock availability is the single most common reason a job runs late. If you have a fixed date, say so at the start and we will only quote garments that can actually make it.

One more thing worth knowing before you order. A decorated garment cannot be returned for a size swap, because it was made for you. For any rollout beyond about fifteen people, ask us about a size set so your team can try on rather than guess.

Artwork for uniforms and merch

Send a vector file, so EPS, AI or PDF with the fonts outlined. A logo pulled off your website will reproduce exactly as blurry as it looks on screen. For DTF and full colour work a high resolution PNG at the actual print size with a transparent background also works well.

Embroidery needs one extra step. Your logo has to be digitised, which is a separate file telling the machine the stitch path and the order it sews in. That is a one off cost per logo and the file is kept on record, so your reorders and your new starters do not pay it again. Fine text under about 5mm high, thin outlines and gradients are the three things that cause trouble in thread, and we will tell you before you commit rather than after.

One thing worth saying plainly. Decoration positions are described from the wearer’s point of view, so left chest means their left, which is your right when you are looking at them. Getting that straight at the start saves a whole reprint.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a custom hoodie?

Outerwear is the widest price range in a uniform order. A basic fleece hoodie with one embroidered chest logo sits well below a heavyweight merch hoodie with a full back print, and a branded puffer sits above both. Tell us the garment, the numbers and the decoration positions and we will quote it accurately.

What is the minimum order?

Around ten to twelve units on a new embroidered logo, and usually lower on reorders once the logo is digitised. Print minimums depend on the method, with DTF workable from small numbers and screen printing needing around twenty to twenty five. Confirmed on your quote.

Should I embroider or print my hoodie?

Embroidery for anything that reads as a uniform, particularly softshell and corporate fleece. Print for merch, large designs, full colour artwork and anything with names or numbers. A lot of orders do both, with an embroidered chest and a printed back.

Can I get hi vis hoodies?

Yes. JB’s make a hi vis trade hoodie in both day only and day and night versions. As with all hi vis, we state what the manufacturer publishes for the specific style and confirm it in writing on the quote, and decoration has to avoid the reflective tape.

Do you do kids and ladies sizes?

Yes on most styles. The JB’s fleecy hoodie has ladies and kids versions, and the Podium softshell runs adults 2XS to 5XL plus a kids run from 4 to 14 with a matching ladies cut. That combination covers school, club and family business orders in one style.

How long do they take?

As a guide, ten to fifteen business days from artwork approval, plus freight. Outerwear is also the category where supplier stock runs out fastest heading into winter, so order earlier than you think you need to.

Can you do names on the back?

Yes. DTF handles individual names and numbers without a separate setup for each garment, which makes it the practical choice for team and school orders. Embroidered names are also possible and are charged per garment.

Ready to kit out the team?

Send us the headcount, the garments you like and your deadline. We will come back with a real number, including freight to your address.
