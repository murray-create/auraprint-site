MEDIA WALLS SUNSHINE COAST

Straight or curved backdrops for events, launches and step-and-repeats.

Backdrop prices without the phone call

A media wall is the backdrop every photo from your event ends up in front of. Choose a size and whether you need the frame, and the price is on screen with GST and standard Australia-wide delivery included.

The graphic is tensioned fabric stretched over a collapsible aluminium frame. Fabric matters here: it does not glare under a camera flash the way a vinyl banner does, it washes, and it packs into a bag instead of a tube. That is why step-and-repeat walls are fabric and not PVC.

Which size?

2000 × 2000mm fits two or three people in shot and suits a corner of a room, a small stand or a shopfront. It is the size most small businesses actually need.

2300 × 2300mm is the comfortable step up. Room for a group without anyone standing outside the branding, and still a one-person carry.

3000 × 2400mm and 3048 × 2438mm are proper event walls. Use them for red carpet style step-and-repeats, award nights, a wall behind a stage, or a full stand back wall at an expo. The 3048 × 2438 is the imperial 10ft by 8ft size, which is what most exhibition stand plans are drawn to.

Designing a wall people photograph well against

  • Repeat the logo, do not centre it. A step-and-repeat works because whoever stands in front of it, and wherever the photographer crops, a whole logo is still in frame. One big centred logo gets blocked by the first person who stands there.
  • Keep the bottom 400mm quiet. People stand in front of it. Anything down there is behind their legs.
  • Avoid fine detail and thin type. Fabric is a slightly softer print than vinyl, and phone cameras in a dim venue are softer again.
  • Mid-tone backgrounds photograph best. Pure white blows out under flash and deep black loses the logo entirely. A mid-tone brand colour holds detail in both.
  • Two logos maximum. Yours and the event's, or yours and a sponsor's. More than that and none of them read.

Setting it up and packing it down

The frame pushes together from sections, the fabric pulls over it like a fitted sheet and zips at the base. Two people and a few minutes with no tools. Take the fabric off for transport, because the frame travels in the bag alongside it.

The graphic washes. Cold hand wash, no bleach, hang it to dry and put it straight back on the frame, and any creases pull out under tension. That is the practical advantage of fabric over vinyl for something that gets handled by a lot of people.

Frequently asked questions

How long to set up?

A few minutes with two people and no tools. The aluminium frame pushes together from sections, the printed fabric pulls over it like a fitted sheet and zips closed at the base. Packing down is the same in reverse and everything goes into one carry bag.

What does a media wall cost?

A 2000 x 2000mm wall with the frame, graphic and carry bag is $483 including GST and delivery. 2300 x 2300mm is $542, 3000 x 2400mm is $628 and 3048 x 2438mm is $638. A replacement graphic on its own starts at $200.

Can I reuse the frame and just reprint the graphic?

Yes, and that is the best value on this page. The frame is adjustable and costs the same across most sizes, so buy it once and every future event costs you only the graphic from $200. Clubs reprint the sponsor wall each season and keep the same frame for years.

What size do I need?

2000 x 2000mm fits two or three people in shot and suits a shopfront or a small stand. 2300 x 2300mm gives you room for a group. 3000 x 2400mm and 3048 x 2438mm are proper event walls for step-and-repeats and stage backdrops. The 3048 x 2438 is the imperial 10ft by 8ft size that most exhibition stand plans are drawn to.

Why fabric instead of a vinyl banner?

Fabric does not glare under a camera flash, which matters enormously for a backdrop that exists to be photographed. It also washes, and it packs into a bag rather than a tube. Creases pull out under tension when you put it back on the frame.

How should I lay out the logos?

Repeat the logo across the whole wall rather than centring one big one. A step-and-repeat works because whoever stands in front of it, and wherever the photographer crops, a complete logo is still in frame. Two different logos is the sensible maximum.

How much of the artwork gets hidden?

The fabric wraps around the frame before it zips, so the outer edge disappears around the back. Allow 50mm bleed and keep anything important 100mm in from every edge. Also keep the lowest 400mm plain, because people stand in front of it.

Can I wash it?

Yes. Cold hand wash, no bleach, hang it to dry, then put it straight back on the frame. That is a real advantage over vinyl for a backdrop handled by a lot of people at a lot of events.

Is it suitable outdoors?

It is designed as an indoor product. A large flat surface acts as a sail, so outdoors it needs to be sheltered and weighted, and we would not recommend it in open wind. For outdoor branding use hemmed vinyl banners, teardrop flags or an A-frame.

Can you print a curved wall?

Yes, curved and straight configurations are both available, and a curve reads as more premium behind a speaker or a product. Tell us the footprint you have to work with and we will quote it.

Do you deliver Australia-wide?

Yes, and standard delivery is included in the prices on this page. We are based at Baringa on the Sunshine Coast and often deliver locally ourselves.

What artwork do you need?

A print-ready PDF in CMYK at 100 to 150dpi final size, fonts outlined, with 50mm bleed. Tell us the exact frame you are ordering and we will send the matching template, since every size has a different wrap allowance.
