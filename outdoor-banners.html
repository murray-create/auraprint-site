OUTDOOR VINYL BANNERS SUNSHINE COAST

Hemmed and eyeletted heavy-duty vinyl, any size, built for weather.

Banner printing with the price up front

Choose a size, choose how you want it finished, and the price is on screen. No form, no wait, no phone tag. Heavy-duty PVC banner vinyl, printed with UV inks, hemmed and finished to suit how you are hanging it.

A vinyl banner is the cheapest large-format advertising there is. It goes up in minutes, comes down in minutes, rolls into a tube and gets used again next season. On the Sunshine Coast the two things that kill banners are wind and salt, so how it is finished matters as much as how it is printed.

Choosing your finish

This is the decision that determines whether your banner survives its first strong southerly.

Hemmed edges, ropes and eyelets. The default and the one we recommend for anything outdoors. The hem doubles the vinyl at the edge, the eyelets give you fixing points, and the rope sewn into the hem spreads the load so the eyelets do not tear out.

Hemmed edges and eyelets. Fine for short campaigns and sheltered positions. Without rope, all the wind load lands on the eyelets.

Hemmed edges and ropes. For tying off along the full edge rather than at fixed points, which is common on fences and scaffolding.

Hemmed edges only. Cheapest. Suitable indoors, or where the banner is being fixed into a frame or track.

Rod pockets, 75mm lay flat. A sewn sleeve top and bottom for a pole or rod to slide through. This is what you want for pole-mounted banners and hanging displays, and it gives the cleanest look because no fixings are visible.

Getting a banner to survive the Coast

  • Wind is the enemy, not rain. A solid banner is a sail. If it is going somewhere exposed, ask us about wind slits, or use fence mesh instead, which lets air through.
  • Fix every eyelet. Tying only the corners on a 3m banner is how banners end up flapping and tearing at the fixing points.
  • Use cable ties or bungee, not wire. A little give absorbs gusts. Wire transfers every gust straight into the hem.
  • Rinse the salt off. Anything within sight of the water picks up salt. A hose-down before you roll it away makes the difference between one season and three.
  • Roll, do not fold. A fold crease in banner vinyl is permanent and it will be the first place it cracks.

Artwork for large format

Banner artwork does not need to be enormous, because nobody reads a banner from arm's length. Supply at a scale with the right proportions and we handle the rest.

Keep text well inside the safe area. The hem folds roughly 25mm of your artwork over, and eyelets punch through the edge, so anything sitting close to the border will be swallowed or perforated.

One rule that matters more than any other: a banner is read at speed, from distance. One message, a huge phone number or web address, and the strongest contrast you can manage. If you need three sentences on it, you need a brochure, not a banner.

Frequently asked questions

What size banners can you print?

2000 x 1000mm, 3000 x 1000mm, 3000 x 1500mm and 4000 x 1000mm all price instantly on this page. We print larger and custom sizes on request, including full building wraps and fence-length runs, which we quote.

How much does a vinyl banner cost?

Ten 2000 x 1000mm banners with hemmed edges, ropes and eyelets are $561, which is $56.10 each. The same run at 3000 x 1000mm is $774, at 4000 x 1000mm is $1,074, and at 3000 x 1500mm is $1,095. Banner pricing starts from $150 including GST and standard delivery.

Which finish should I choose?

Hemmed edges with ropes and eyelets for anything outdoors, because the rope spreads the wind load so the eyelets do not tear out. Hemmed and eyelets is fine for short campaigns in sheltered spots. Rod pockets are for pole-mounted and hanging banners, and give the cleanest look because no fixings show.

How long will a banner last outside?

A well-fixed banner in a normal position will give you a few seasons. What shortens that life is wind damage at the fixing points and salt if you are near the water. Rinse it before you store it, roll it rather than folding it, and it will come out looking good next time.

Will it survive strong wind?

A solid banner is a sail, and wind is what destroys banners, not rain. For exposed positions ask us about wind slits, or use fence mesh instead, which lets air through. Fix every eyelet rather than just the corners, and use cable ties or bungee rather than wire so there is a little give.

How many eyelets do I get?

Usually one every 500mm around the hemmed edge. If you need them in specific positions to suit a frame or existing fixings, tell us and mark them on your artwork.

What resolution does my artwork need to be?

100 to 150dpi at final size is plenty, or supply at quarter size and 300dpi. Nobody reads a banner from arm's length. Set it in CMYK, allow 10mm bleed, and keep all text at least 100mm in from the edges so the hem and eyelets do not eat it.

How fast can I get a banner?

Standard production is 3 to 5 business days from artwork approval, then delivery. Every effort is made to get your job onto the press for the dispatch shown. On the odd occasion a press or freight delay pushes it out, and we will tell you as soon as we know. If you have a hard event deadline, tell us the date up front.

Do you deliver on the Sunshine Coast?

Yes, and Australia-wide by courier. Local Sunshine Coast deliveries we often run out ourselves. We do not offer pickup from Baringa.

Can you print double sided banners?

Yes, we quote double sided banners on request. They use a blockout vinyl so the reverse image does not ghost through. Tell us the size and quantity and we will price it.

Can I reuse a banner if my details change?

Not the printed part, no. If you expect the message to change, keep the dates and prices off the banner and put them on a corflute sign next to it, which is cheap to reprint.

What is the difference between a banner and fence mesh?

Banner vinyl is solid, so it looks better and blocks the view behind it. Fence mesh is perforated, so wind passes through it and it will not act as a sail. On long construction fences and anything very exposed, mesh is the safer choice.
