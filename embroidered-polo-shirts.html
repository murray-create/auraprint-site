EMBROIDERED POLO SHIRTS

The polo is the garment most Sunshine Coast businesses put their logo on first. It works on a building site, behind a counter and at a client meeting, it survives a commercial wash, and it does not need ironing. We carry a core range from the major Australian workwear brands, in mens, ladies and kids fits, with size runs that go from 2XS all the way to 13XL.

Choosing the right work polo

Poly cotton or full polyester

A 65/35 poly cotton pique like the JB’s 210 or the Biz Collection P400MS is the default for a reason. It holds its shape through years of washing, it does not shrink much, and embroidery sits flat on it. Full polyester mesh polos like the Biz Collection Sprint or the JB’s Podium are lighter and move sweat off the skin faster, which matters in a Queensland summer or on an outdoor crew. The trade off is that polyester feels more like sportswear and less like a uniform, so it suits trades, clubs and events better than a front counter.

Size runs are the thing people underestimate

A uniform programme lives or dies on whether you can fit everybody. The JB’s 210 family runs from 2XS to 13XL in mens, 6 to 30 in ladies and 2 to 14 in kids, which is why it wins most staff rollouts. If you have one person who needs a 7XL, choosing a style that stops at 3XL means either that person wears something different or you change the whole order. Check the top and bottom of the range before you commit, not after.

Embroidery is almost always the right call on a polo

A pique knit has texture, so a printed logo sits on top of it and can crack or fade over time. Embroidery goes into the fabric and outlasts the garment. The standard position is the left chest, meaning the wearer’s left, at around 80mm to 100mm wide. Add a second logo on the right chest, a name above or below the logo, or the business name across the upper back where you want people to read it from a distance. Each position is a separate setup, so decide them all before you order.

Mens, ladies and youth fits are not the same garment

A ladies polo is not just a smaller mens polo. The shoulder, the sleeve and the body shaping are all different, and so is the placket. Most brands in this range publish a matched pair, for example the JB’s 210 and the 2LPS, or the Biz Collection P400MS and P400LS, so a mixed team gets the same colour and the same fabric in the right cut. Ordering everybody the same unisex garment is cheaper on paper and is the most common reason a uniform order gets worn once and left in a drawer.

Who on the Coast wears these

Trades and construction

Plumbers, sparkies, builders and landscapers. Usually a poly cotton polo for office and quoting days, with hi vis for site. Left chest logo, business name across the back.

Cafes and hospitality

Front of house in a contrast polo or a softer cotton rich pique, often matched to an apron. Small, clean left chest embroidery reads better than a large logo here.

Real estate and property

Agents and property managers at open homes. Corporate polos in the agency colours, frequently with the agent name embroidered under the logo.

Clubs and community sport

Committee shirts, coaching staff and volunteers. Sports mesh in club colours, and the kids size run matters as much as the adults one.

Allied health and clinics

Physios, dentists, vets and mobile practitioners. Plain corporate polos in a single colour, with a small logo and sometimes a role title.

Councils, schools and not for profits

Event days, groundskeeping and admin staff. Tender and procurement work tends to ask about fabric composition and sun protection, so those details are worth having up front.

How ordering works

Tell us what you need

Pick the garments below and send them through, or just describe the job. Headcount, rough sizes, how many logo positions and your deadline are enough to start.

You get a visual showing your logo in position and at actual size on the garment you chose. Nothing is stitched or pressed until you approve it.

How we supply uniforms

We buy the garments ourselves from the major Australian workwear and apparel brands, so we can put you in the right garment rather than the one brand we happen to stock. DTF transfers are pressed in-house. The full colour film is printed for us by a trade printer, and we press, check and pack every garment ourselves. Embroidery and screen printing are done by an established Sunshine Coast decoration house we work with. Either way you deal with us from quote to delivery.

That is deliberate. It means a small order is not penalised for being small, and a size top up or a new starter’s shirt does not have to wait for a big production run. It also means we quote rather than show a live price on these pages, because the honest number depends on the garment, the quantity, the number of decoration positions and how many stitches your logo needs. As a guide, embroidery starts from $15.50 a garment inc GST at the lowest stitch count, plus the garment.

Minimums and lead times, honestly

Every number on this page is a guide, and we confirm the real one on your quote. As a rough guide: embroidery from around 10 to 12 units on a new logo and often a single garment on a reorder, screen printing from around 20 to 25 per design per colour, and DTF from about 10 with no real technical minimum. Caps usually hold a minimum of around 12 even on repeats.

Lead time is typically 10 to 15 business days from the moment you approve the artwork, plus freight to you. Two things get missed. The clock starts at artwork approval and not at order placement, and garment stock availability is the single most common reason a job runs late. If you have a fixed date, say so at the start and we will only quote garments that can actually make it.

One more thing worth knowing before you order. A decorated garment cannot be returned for a size swap, because it was made for you. For any rollout beyond about fifteen people, ask us about a size set so your team can try on rather than guess.

Artwork for uniforms and merch

Send a vector file, so EPS, AI or PDF with the fonts outlined. A logo pulled off your website will reproduce exactly as blurry as it looks on screen. For DTF and full colour work a high resolution PNG at the actual print size with a transparent background also works well.

Embroidery needs one extra step. Your logo has to be digitised, which is a separate file telling the machine the stitch path and the order it sews in. That is a one off cost per logo and the file is kept on record, so your reorders and your new starters do not pay it again. Fine text under about 5mm high, thin outlines and gradients are the three things that cause trouble in thread, and we will tell you before you commit rather than after.

One thing worth saying plainly. Decoration positions are described from the wearer’s point of view, so left chest means their left, which is your right when you are looking at them. Getting that straight at the start saves a whole reprint.

Frequently asked questions

How much do embroidered polo shirts cost?

It depends on the garment, the quantity, how many decoration positions you want and how many stitches your logo needs. As a guide, embroidery starts from $15.50 a garment inc GST at the lowest stitch count, plus the garment. A plain poly cotton polo with a single left chest logo sits at the affordable end. A premium branded polo with a chest logo, a back print and names on each shirt sits well above it. Send us the logo, the rough numbers and the garment you like and we will price it properly rather than quote you a range that turns out to be wrong.

What is the minimum order for embroidered polos?

As a guide, around ten to twelve units on a new logo, because the logo has to be digitised first and that cost has to sit somewhere. Once your logo is digitised and on file, reorders can usually go down to a single garment. These are indicative and we confirm the real minimum for your garment and decorator on the quote.

How long do they take?

As a guide, ten to fifteen business days from the moment you approve the artwork, plus freight. Two things are worth knowing. The clock starts at artwork approval, not when you place the order, and supplier stock is the single most common cause of a delay. If you have a hard deadline, tell us at the start and we will only quote you garments that can actually make it.

Can I mix mens, ladies and kids sizes in one order?

Yes, and you should. Most of the ranges here publish a matched mens, ladies and kids style in the same fabric and the same colours, so everyone gets the right cut without the order looking mismatched. The decoration stays identical across all of them.

What file do you need for my logo?

A vector file is best, so EPS, AI or PDF with the fonts outlined. For embroidery your logo also has to be digitised, which is a separate file that tells the machine the stitch path. That is a one off fee of $68 inc GST per logo, and we keep the file so reorders do not pay it again. If all you have is a logo off your website, send it anyway and we will tell you what it needs.

Will my logo embroider properly?

Most do. The things that cause trouble are fine text under about 5mm high, thin outlines, gradients and drop shadows. Embroidery is thread, so it cannot fade one colour into another the way a print can. If your logo needs simplifying for the chest position we will tell you before you commit, and you will see it on a mockup at actual size first.

Can you embroider garments I already own?

Sometimes, but it depends on the garment and the decorator. Decorating customer supplied stock carries a risk, because if a garment is damaged in the hooping there is no replacement to draw on. Ask us before you buy the stock rather than after.

Ready to kit out the team?

Send us the headcount, the garments you like and your deadline. We will come back with a real number, including freight to your address.
